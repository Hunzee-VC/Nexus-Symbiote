# Feature enhancement plan, 18 November 2026

What would take Nexus SymBiote from the AIBOM-Bench leader to the AI-BOM tool no other tool matches: the competitor advantages to close, the standards and regulatory fields it could evidence, the gaps no tool fills, what the maintainer may have missed, and a prioritized roadmap with acceptance criteria written as benchmark cases.

- **Basis.** The AIBOM-Bench results in [`benchmarks/aibom-bench`](../benchmarks/aibom-bench/README.md) ([specification](../benchmarks/aibom-bench/SPEC.md), [report](../benchmarks/aibom-bench/REPORT.md), [critique](../benchmarks/aibom-bench/CRITIQUE.md), [audit](../benchmarks/aibom-bench/AUDIT.md)), the nine competitor source trees at the versions the report records, the vendored CycloneDX 1.7 and SPDX 3.0.1 schemas, and this repository at `eb6a853`. The [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) is the baseline: an item it already lists is marked as planned, with what to change.
- **How it was produced.** One mapping pass over the collectors, extension points, constraints and roadmap; one comparison per competitor (215 advantages, 136 where Nexus is partial and 78 where it is absent); four standards sweeps; a six-perspective panel (red team, EU AI Act auditor, platform engineer, ML engineer, CISO buyer, protocol specialist) that proposed 69 gaps, each then tried by two independent skeptics, one checking the ten competitor sources for prior art and one checking feasibility inside the Nexus constraints and value to a buyer. 29 proposals survived both, 19 survived one, 21 were refuted. A completeness critic then read the synthesized plan against every input; its 35 findings and what was done about each are the last section, and the corrections it forced are applied in the text above them.
- **Evidence.** Nexus pointers are `file:line` in this tree; competitor pointers are `file:line` in the source trees the report names; benchmark numbers come from `results/scores.json`, `results/corpus-b/scores.json` and `grades.json`. About eighty Nexus pointers were re-checked by hand on 18 November 2026, forty from the synthesis and forty from the critic; three from the synthesis were corrected (the live MCP protocol list is in `scanners/live_mcp.py:66`, the tool definition hash is written at `scanners/mcp.py:3232-3236`, and only `destructiveHint` is read at `scanners/mcp.py:3331-3348`), and the critic's held. Where the inputs disagree, both readings are kept and said to disagree.
- **Status.** A proposal, not a commitment. The open questions at the end are the decisions only the maintainer can take.

## Where Nexus stands

Nexus SymBiote leads AIBOM-Bench at 73.0 (B; REPORT.md:158 still prints the superseded 79.7, which the banner at REPORT.md:3-7 retracts) because it is the only tool the benchmark could verify as safe, honest and deterministic: zero egress, zero process creation, no symlink opens, failures and limits recorded inside the inventory, byte-identical repeats, valid CycloneDX 1.7, SPDX 3.0.1, SARIF and VEX, a G7 minimum-elements report, SHA-256 on every model file and attested releases (benchmarks/aibom-bench/REPORT.md:160-169; AUDIT.md:75). One sentence of that report is wrong and the plan does not rely on it: REPORT.md:163-164 says the SPDX export carries the AI and Dataset profiles, while generators/spdx.py:1-9 says those profiles are not claimed because their mandatory properties are not collected. It is not yet the best inventory: it reads the agent surface it documents and almost nothing else, so the second corpus, built from standards and competitors' READMEs rather than Nexus's docs, cut its D1 from 1.75 to 0.75 (grades.json D1, D1_B) while the safety, provenance, standards and determinism dimensions did not move. Becoming number one means closing the coverage gap without giving back any of the properties that produced the lead.
